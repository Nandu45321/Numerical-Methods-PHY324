Numerical Methods: Theory Questionnaire

Covers Lectures 1–8 (root finding, interpolation, splines, integration, Gaussian quadrature, linear and nonlinear systems) plus the LU and Newton-system notebooks. ★★ = medium, ★★★ = hard. Prove or justify every claim; no numerical answers are wanted unless stated.
Part A: Root Finding (Lectures 1–2)

A1 ★★ Show that after n bisection steps the interval has length (b−a)/2ⁿ. Find the least n guaranteeing error < TOL. Why is this bound independent of f? The interval bound halves every step, but must the actual error |c_n − r| decrease monotonically? Explain. What does f(a)f(b) < 0 guarantee when the interval holds several roots?

A2 ★★ Derive Newton–Raphson in two ways: from a truncated Taylor series and from tangent-line geometry. State precisely what is being approximated in each derivation.

A3 ★★★ Using Taylor's theorem with Lagrange remainder, prove e_{i+1} = e_i² · f″(c_i)/(2f′(x_i)) and lim |e_{i+1}|/e_i² = |f″(r)/(2f′(r))|. List every hypothesis used silently (smoothness, f′(r) ≠ 0, closeness of x₀). Which one fails at a multiple root?

A4 ★★★ Let f(x) = (x−r)^m Q(x) with Q(r) ≠ 0. Show this is equivalent to f(r) = … = f^(m−1)(r) = 0, f^(m)(r) ≠ 0. Write Newton's method as a fixed-point iteration g(x) = x − f/f′ and prove g′(r) = 1 − 1/m, so convergence is linear with S = (m−1)/m for every such f, not only f = x^m.

A5 ★★★ Prove the Modified Newton method x_{i+1} = x_i − m f(x_i)/f′(x_i) converges quadratically at a root of multiplicity m. m is usually unknown; propose a way to estimate it from a run of ordinary Newton iterates, and discuss its reliability under round-off.

A6 ★★ Describe two distinct failure mechanisms of Newton's method (e.g. f′(x_i) = 0, and the 2-cycle for f = 4x⁴ − 6x² − 11/4 from x₀ = ½). Explain each from the tangent-line picture and say why convergence is "initial-condition dependent".

A7 ★★★ The margin note in Lecture 1 replaces f′ by the central difference [f(x+h) − f(x−h)]/2h, with O(h²) error. Analyse how this perturbs the order of Newton's method for (i) fixed h and (ii) h chosen as a function of the current error e_i. How should h scale to retain quadratic convergence, and what does round-off impose from below?

A8 ★★★ Derive e_{i+1} ≈ C e_i e_{i−1}, C = f″(r)/(2f′(r)), for the secant method. Then, using the ansatz |e_{i+1}| ≈ K|e_i|^α, derive α² − α − 1 = 0. Explain the margin question "golden ratio??": show that log|e_i| obeys a Fibonacci-type recurrence.

A9 ★★★ Compare Newton (order 2, two evaluations f and f′ per step) and secant (order ≈1.618, one evaluation per step) using the efficiency index p^(1/w). Which is cheaper per correct digit? Under what circumstances does the answer reverse?

A10 ★★★ Inverse Quadratic Interpolation fits x = P(y) rather than y = P(x). Show the next iterate is P(0). Why does direct quadratic interpolation in x cause trouble (no real root, or two roots)? Give a configuration where IQI breaks down, and explain why practical solvers pair it with bisection.
Part B: Polynomial Interpolation (Lecture 3)

B1 ★★ Prove the Main Theorem of Polynomial Interpolation (existence and uniqueness of the degree ≤ n−1 polynomial through n points with distinct x_i). Use l_i(x_j) = δ_ij for existence. Where does the uniqueness proof break if two nodes coincide?

B2 ★★ The monomial (Vandermonde), Lagrange, and Newton forms represent the same polynomial. Compare construction cost, evaluation cost, and the cost of adding one more node. Why is the Vandermonde system typically ill-conditioned?

B3 ★★★ Prove Σᵢ l_i(x) ≡ 1, and more generally Σᵢ x_i^k l_i(x) = x^k for 0 ≤ k ≤ n−1. Interpret the identity as a statement about which functions the interpolation operator reproduces.

B4 ★★★ Follow the P₁/P₂ argument in the notes to prove that f[x₁…x_k] is symmetric in its arguments and satisfies f[x₁…x_k] = (f[x₂…x_k] − f[x₁…x_{k−1}])/(x_k − x₁). Which step uses uniqueness?

B5 ★★★ Prove f[x₁…x_n] is the coefficient of x^(n−1) in the interpolating polynomial. Combine this with the interpolation-error argument to show f[x₁…x_n] = f^(n−1)(c)/(n−1)! for some c. What does this suggest about divided differences when nodes coalesce, and how does it lead to Hermite interpolation?

B6 ★★★ In the Rolle's-theorem proof of the error formula, justify: (i) h(t) has n+1 zeros, (ii) h^(n) has at least one zero, (iii) the nth derivative of P_{n−1} vanishes. Where is smoothness of f needed? What does the resulting bound say, and not say, about convergence as n → ∞?

B7 ★★ Let f be a polynomial of degree ≤ n−1, interpolated at n distinct nodes. Give two independent arguments (uniqueness, and the error formula) that the error is zero.

B8 ★★ A degree-d polynomial has d+1 free coefficients. Use this counting argument to decide: does a degree-6 polynomial exist that is 0 at x = 1,…,6, equals 10 at 7, and equals 70 at 8? What if the value at 8 were 71? Explain the structure of all degree-6 polynomials vanishing at 1,…,6.
Part C: Runge Phenomenon, Chebyshev Nodes, Splines (Lecture 4)

C1 ★★ Using the error formula, identify which factor causes the Runge phenomenon for equispaced nodes and explain why it is large near the endpoints. Is the failure due to the interpolation process, the function, or both? (Hint: consider the poles of 1/(1+12x²) in the complex plane.)

C2 ★★★ State Chebyshev's min-max theorem for monic polynomials on [−1, 1]. Reproduce the proof: why is T_n/2^(n−1) monic, why does P_n − T_n/2^(n−1) change sign n times, why is its degree ≤ n−1, and where is the strict inequality used?

C3 ★★ Show T_n(x) = cos(n cos⁻¹x) is a polynomial by deriving T_{n+1} = 2xT_n − T_{n−1}. Deduce the degree n, the leading coefficient 2^(n−1), the roots cos((2i−1)π/2n), and the n+1 alternating extrema. For a general interval [a, b], derive the shifted nodes and the value of max|∏(x − x_i)|.

C4 ★★★ With Chebyshev nodes derive max|f − P_{n−1}| ≤ M_n/(2^(n−1) n!) on [−1, 1]. Does this bound alone prove uniform convergence for f = 1/(1+12x²)? Why or why not?

C5 ★★ A cubic spline on n nodes has 3(n−1) unknowns once y_i is built into each piece. Reconcile this with the 3n−5 equations from Properties 1–3. What extra conditions close the system (natural, clamped, not-a-knot), and what does each mean physically?

C6 ★★★ Derive b_i = Δ_i/δ_i − c_iδ_i − d_iδ_i² and d_i = (c_{i+1} − c_i)/(3δ_i), then the tridiagonal system for the c_i. Show the natural-spline matrix is strictly diagonally dominant, hence the spline exists and is unique (connect to Lecture 8).

C7 ★★★ (extension) Show the natural cubic spline minimises ∫(f″)² among all C² interpolants of the same data. Use integration by parts. This is the sense in which it is the "smoothest" interpolant.
