Numerical Methods: Quiz 2 Theory Questionnaire

Covers:
- Finite Difference Schemes and Boundary Conditions
- 1D and 2D Diffusion Equations (FTCS, BTCS, Fourier mode decay, Heterogeneous D(x))
- 1D First-Order and Second-Order Wave Equations (Upwind, Leap-Frog, CFL condition, Exactness)
- Stochastic Systems: 1D and 2D Random Walks, Realization vs. Ensemble Average, Langevin Dynamics
- Fokker-Planck (Smoluchowski) Equation and Gibbs-Boltzmann Equilibrium
- Advanced ODE Systems: Explicit vs. Implicit Euler (Nuclear Decay), Damped/Driven Oscillators (Euler, Euler-Cromer, RK4), Nonlinear Systems (Fixed Points & Phase Portraits), Multivariable Newton's Method.

★★ = medium difficulty, ★★★ = hard / derivation intensive.
Prove or justify every claim; no numerical answers are wanted unless stated.

======================================================================
Part A: Finite Differences and Boundary Conditions
======================================================================

A1 ★★ Using Taylor expansions of f(x + Δx) and f(x - Δx), derive the central difference approximations for f'(x) and f''(x). What are their respective leading-order truncation errors? Why is the central difference for the first derivative of order O(Δx^2) whereas forward and backward differences are only O(Δx)?

A2 ★★ In class notes, the second derivative is alternatively discretized as nested differences:
d^2f/dx^2 ≈ (1/Δx) [ (f(x_{i+1}) - f(x_i))/Δx - (f(x_i) - f(x_{i-1}))/Δx ].
Show that this gives the exact same three-point stencil. Interpret this geometrically as the difference between adjacent slopes.

A3 ★★ Define the three standard boundary condition types for PDEs on [0, L]:
(i) Dirichlet B.C.
(ii) Neumann (No-flux) B.C.
(iii) Periodic B.C.
For a diffusion process ∂ρ/∂t = D ∂^2ρ/∂x^2, use Fick's law J = -D ∂ρ/∂x and the continuity equation to show why Neumann boundary conditions (∂ρ/∂x = 0 at boundaries) guarantee strict conservation of the total number of particles N(t) = ∫_0^L ρ(x, t) dx. How are ghost points or boundary nodes implemented in discrete code?

A4 ★★ What are physical symmetries in PDEs (e.g., translational invariance x -> x + a and parity reflection x -> -x)? How do boundary conditions break these symmetries, and what does this imply for the steady-state solution?

Part B: Diffusion Equations (Parabolic PDEs)
======================================================================

B1 ★★★ Formulate the Forward Time Centered Space (FTCS) explicit scheme for the 1D diffusion equation ∂ρ/∂t = D ∂^2ρ/∂x^2. Perform a complete, step-by-step von Neumann stability analysis by substituting a single spatial Fourier mode ρ_j^n = T^n e^{i k x_j}. Derive the amplification factor G(k) = 1 - 4C sin^2(k Δx / 2), where C = D Δt / Δx^2. Hence, prove that the scheme is stable if and only if C ≤ 1/2. What happens to the numerical solution when C > 1/2?

B2 ★★★ Consider the diffusion equation in Fourier space: ∂\hat{ρ}(k, t)/∂t = -D k^2 \hat{ρ}(k, t).
(i) Show that each mode decays as exp(-D k^2 t), with characteristic relaxation timescale τ_k = 1 / (D k^2) = λ^2 / (4 π^2 D).
(ii) Explain physically why higher wavenumber modes (shorter wavelengths) equilibrate much faster than lower wavenumber modes.
(iii) In Problem Set III Q6, compare the relaxation times of an initial profile with k_1 = 2π/L versus k_2 = 16π/L. By what exact factor does the second profile homogenize faster?

B3 ★★★ Derive the Backward Time Centered Space (BTCS) implicit scheme for the 1D diffusion equation. Write down the resulting tridiagonal system A ρ^{n+1} = ρ^n. Using von Neumann stability analysis, derive the amplification factor G(k) for the BTCS scheme and prove that it is unconditionally stable for any Δt > 0, Δx > 0. Why is BTCS preferred over FTCS for long-time simulations despite requiring a matrix solve at every time step?

B4 ★★★ Consider the two-dimensional diffusion equation ∂ρ/∂t = D (∂^2ρ/∂x^2 + ∂^2ρ/∂y^2) on a grid with uniform spacing Δx = Δy = δ.
(i) Write down the FTCS discretization in 2D.
(ii) Perform von Neumann stability analysis and prove that the stability condition is D Δt / δ^2 ≤ 1/4.
(iii) Generalize this result to d spatial dimensions: prove that the stability criterion is D Δt / δ^2 ≤ 1 / (2d).

B5 ★★★ In a heterogeneous medium with spatially varying diffusion coefficient D(x), the diffusion equation is ∂ρ/∂t = ∂/∂x [ D(x) ∂ρ/∂x ].
(i) Why does naive expansion D(x) ∂^2ρ/∂x^2 + D'(x) ∂ρ/∂x fail to conserve particle flux numerically?
(ii) Derive the conservative finite-difference scheme using half-grid diffusivities D_{i ± 1/2}.
(iii) Analytically derive the steady-state concentration profile under no-flux and periodic boundary conditions.

Part C: Wave and Advection Equations (Hyperbolic PDEs)
======================================================================

C1 ★★★ Consider the 1D first-order wave (advection) equation ∂ψ/∂t + c ∂ψ/∂x = 0 with c > 0.
(i) Show that the naive central difference in space with forward difference in time is unconditionally unstable for all Δt > 0.
(ii) Formulate the upwind scheme for c > 0 using a backward difference in space.
(iii) Perform von Neumann stability analysis on the upwind scheme and derive the Courant-Friedrichs-Lewy (CFL) condition: c Δt / Δx ≤ 1.
(iv) If c < 0, which spatial difference must be chosen to maintain stability? Explain the physical meaning of "upwind".

C2 ★★★ Using modified equation analysis (Taylor expansion of the discrete difference equations), show that the upwind scheme introduces an artificial numerical diffusion term D_num ∂^2ψ/∂x^2, where D_num = (c Δx / 2)(1 - c Δt / Δx). What happens when the Courant number c Δt / Δx = 1?

C3 ★★★ Consider the 1D second-order wave equation ∂^2ψ/∂t^2 = c^2 ∂^2ψ/∂x^2.
(i) Derive the explicit central-in-time, central-in-space discretization.
(ii) Derive the CFL stability condition k = c Δt / Δx ≤ 1.
(iii) Prove that at the "magic" time step k = 1, the discretization truncation error is identically zero, and the scheme propagates waves with zero numerical dispersion.
(iv) The scheme is a three-time-level recurrence requiring ψ^0 and ψ^1. Given the initial position ψ(x, 0) = f(x) and initial velocity ∂ψ/∂t(x, 0) = v(x), derive the second-order accurate starting step for ψ^1 using Taylor expansion.
(v) How do you specify the initial velocity to generate a pure leftward traveling wave without pulse splitting?

Part D: Stochastic Systems, Brownian Motion, and Fokker-Planck
======================================================================

D1 ★★ Consider a 1D discrete random walk x_n = x_{n-1} + η_n, where steps η_n are independent random variables taking values +1 and -1 with probabilities p_r and p_l (p_r + p_l = 1).
(i) For an unbiased walk (p_r = p_l = 1/2), prove analytically that <x_n> = 0 and <x_n^2> = n.
(ii) Show that the continuous limit yields the macroscopic diffusion relation <x(t)^2> = 2 D t, and identify D in terms of the step size Δx and time step Δt.
(iii) For a biased walk (p_r ≠ p_l), derive expressions for the drift velocity <x_n>/t and the shifted variance <(x_n - <x_n>)^2>.
(iv) Explain the fundamental difference between a single stochastic realization and an ensemble average over N_re trajectories.

D2 ★★ For an isotropic 2D random walk where the displacement at step n is R_n = x_n î + y_n ĵ:
(i) Analytically calculate <R_n> and <R_n^2>.
(ii) Compare the result with 1D random walk and generalize to d dimensions.

D3 ★★★ Write down the Langevin equation for a 1D Brownian particle: m dv/dt = -γ v + ξ(t).
(i) State the statistical properties of the Gaussian white noise force ξ(t) (mean and autocorrelation).
(ii) State the Einstein relation relating the diffusion coefficient D to temperature T and friction γ, and explain its origin in the fluctuation-dissipation theorem.
(iii) Write down the Euler-Maruyama discretization algorithm used to numerically integrate the particle's velocity and position.

D4 ★★★ Derive the Fokker-Planck (Smoluchowski) equation from the continuity equation ∂ρ/∂t + ∂J/∂x = 0.
(i) State the physical origins of the two terms in the total flux J = J_diffusion + J_drift for a particle subject to an external potential U(x).
(ii) Write down the resulting PDE for ρ(x, t).
(iii) Prove that the stationary equilibrium solution (J = 0) is the Gibbs-Boltzmann distribution ρ_eq(x) ∝ exp(-U(x) / k_B T).
(iv) Describe how periodic boundary conditions are implemented when discretizing the potential drift term.

Part E: Advanced ODE Systems and Nonlinear Solvers
======================================================================

E1 ★★ Consider the radioactive decay equation dy/dt = -λ y with initial condition y(0) = y_0.
(i) Discretize the equation using explicit Forward Euler and implicit Backward Euler.
(ii) Derive the stability conditions on Δt for both methods.
(iii) Identify the three distinct behavioral regimes of explicit Euler (smooth decay, oscillatory decay, unbounded blowup).

E2 ★★ For a damped harmonic oscillator m x'' + γ x' + k x = 0:
(i) Convert the second-order equation into a system of two coupled first-order ODEs.
(ii) Identify the characteristic timescales of the system (oscillation period, damping time).
(iii) Explain why the simple Forward Euler method fails for undamped or lightly damped harmonic oscillators, whereas the Euler-Cromer (semi-implicit) method succeeds.
(iv) In the strongly overdamped regime (m/(γ^2/k) << 1), justify dropping the second-order inertial term and reducing the system to a first-order ODE.

E3 ★★ For a 2D linear autonomous system dX/dt = J X, where J is a 2x2 constant matrix with trace τ and determinant Δ:
(i) State the eigenvalue equation in terms of τ and Δ.
(ii) Classify the stability and geometric character of the fixed point (0, 0) in the (τ, Δ) plane (saddle, node, spiral, center).
(iii) Which case exhibits a failure of the simple Forward Euler method even though the continuous system is stable? Explain why.

E4 ★★★ State Newton's method for solving a system of coupled nonlinear equations F(x) = 0.
(i) State the multidimensional Taylor expansion leading to the iteration formula x_{k+1} = x_k - [J(x_k)]^{-1} F(x_k).
(ii) What is the theoretical order of convergence near a simple root?
(iii) In practice, why do numerical linear algebra libraries solve J s_k = -F(x_k) via LU/Gaussian elimination instead of explicitly computing the matrix inverse? Why might the explicit inverse formula nevertheless be retained in coursework reference formulations?
